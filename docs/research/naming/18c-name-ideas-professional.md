Temporary notes (git-ignored). Third pass, after `name-ideas.md` (draft) and `name-ideas-review.md` (review). I used both only as input and re-checked their key facts. This is not legal advice.

## Bottom line

1. **PixelLab Trove**: first under every weight set I tried. Main risk: many non-native speakers don't know the word "trove", and Trove is also a known MMO game.
2. **PixelLab Backpack**: a close second. Main risk: the large Backpack crypto wallet and exchange, and the word says "storage" more than "gallery".
3. **PixelLab Shelf**: tied with Backpack on points. Main risk: it is the most "feature-like" word of the three, so it sits closest to looking like an official PixelLab page. A tiny "Pixel Shelf" image-saver extension also exists.

Places 2 to 5 (Backpack 76, Shelf 75, Binder 75, Spritebook 74) are within 2 points of each other, which is inside the margin of my own judgment. Only Trove's lead (84) is clear.

**Changes since the previous review** (Shelf, Trove, Museum, Folio, Binder):

- **Trove moves up to #1, and Shelf drops to #3.** Neumeier's criteria give separate credit for distinctiveness, likability and extendability. Trove scores at or near the top on all three. Shelf is plain, and "shelf" is a common interface word.
- **Backpack moves back into the top 3.** The previous review counted length and tone heavily against it. Neumeier counts brevity as only one of seven criteria. Backpack is also the easiest word in the set to say worldwide, and the one that stretches furthest to new features.
- **Museum and Folio drop out of the top 5.** The trademark screen found a live pixel-art game called *Pixel Museum* (2025–26) and a gallery app called *PixFolio*. Both words also sit near the weak, descriptive end of the naming spectrum.
- **Binder stays close behind, at #4.** Spritebook is the dark horse: it has the cleanest trademark screen, and its .app domain and GitHub handle are both free. Its weaknesses are that it is long and limited to sprites.

## 1. Naming spectrum

**Sources:**

- Trademark law ranks names from weakest to strongest: generic, descriptive, suggestive, arbitrary, fanciful. This scale comes from *Abercrombie & Fitch v. Hunting World* (2d Cir. 1976) ([opinion](https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/tfisher/IP/1976_Abercrombie_Abridged.pdf)).
- The USPTO says today that "strong" trademarks are fanciful, arbitrary or suggestive, and "weak" ones are descriptive or generic ([USPTO, Strong trademarks](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks)).
- Naming agency Igor uses a similar scale, from functional to invented, experiential and evocative names. Igor calls functional names the "lowest common denominator", and treats evocative names as the most engaging ([Igor name taxonomy](https://igorinternational.com/process/naming-company-names-taxonomy.php)).
- Suggestive names make people remember a matching benefit better. The trade-off is that they make later repositioning harder ([Keller, Heckler & Houston 1998, *Journal of Marketing*](https://www.msi.org/working-papers/the-effects-of-brand-name-suggestiveness-on-advertising-recall/); older than 2015, but still the standard study).
- Law-firm guides call suggestive names the best balance between memorability and legal strength ([TM Express](https://tmexpress.com/suggestive-trademarks/)).

| Word | Class, for a gallery app | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Navigator | Descriptive / functional | Says exactly what the app does. This is the same pattern as PixelLab's own product names. |
| Folio | Descriptive, leaning suggestive | "Portfolio" is close to a plain description of an art collection. |
| Museum | Descriptive, leaning suggestive | "A place to view art" is nearly literal. |
| Spritebook | Suggestive, leaning descriptive | A made-up compound, but you can read it as "a book of sprites". |
| Shelf, Binder, Cabinet | Suggestive | Storage or display metaphors. They are common words, so they read close to UI or feature terms. |
| Trove, Stash, Keepsake, Curio, Haul, Reel | Suggestive | Each one hints at "valued collection" or "playback" without saying "gallery". |
| Backpack, Satchel | Suggestive, leaning arbitrary | Needs the game-inventory leap ("bag = my items"). |
| Zoetrope | Arbitrary or fanciful in practice | A real word, but most people won't know it. |

**How the forced "PixelLab" prefix changes things:**

- **"PixelLab" is someone else's brand.** You can't own it, so all of *your* distinctiveness lives in the second word.
- **The prefix already says what category the app is in.** The second word doesn't need to describe anything. That makes a suggestive or arbitrary second word cheaper than usual, because people get the context for free.
- **A descriptive second word is weak, and also risky here.** Brand plus a descriptive noun is exactly how PixelLab names its own products (Editor, Game Builder, Map Workshop), so it looks official. In USPTO practice, descriptive add-ons inside a composite mark are typically disclaimed as non-source-identifying ([TMEP §1213.03(a)](https://www.bitlaw.com/source/tmep/1213-03-a.html)). In practice, nothing in "PixelLab Navigator" would be yours.
- **Result:** suggestive is the sweet spot. Descriptive words (Navigator, and to a lesser extent Folio and Museum) are the weakest choices.

## 2. Neumeier's seven criteria

**Source:**

- *The Brand Gap* says good names are "distinctive, short, appropriate, easy to spell and pronounce, likable, extendable, and protectable". The book is from 2003 (revised 2005). The quote is from [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/blog/whats-in-a-name/) (2012); I didn't read the book itself.
- Secondary sources explain "distinctive" as standing out from rivals and from ordinary words in a sentence, and "short" as at most about four syllables ([Mark Ramsey Media](https://www.markramseymedia.com/amp/what-makes-a-good-radio-station-name)).

How I scored each criterion for this brief (1 is poor, 5 is best):

- **D, Distinctive:** stands apart from rivals *and* from PixelLab's official naming pattern.
- **B, Brevity:** length and syllables of the second word, which is also the home-screen label.
- **A, Appropriate:** signals "gallery / your collection", and ideally animation too.
- **S, Spelling and pronunciation:** for a worldwide, often non-native audience. Uses the linguistic screen in section 4.
- **L, Likable:** warmth and imagery. Lowered for negative meanings.
- **E, Extendable:** still fits if the app grows (section 7).
- **P, Protectable:** trademark knockout results and conflicts (section 5).

**Why some scores are low:**

- **Trove, S 3:** the word is not in the Oxford 5000 learner list, so many non-native speakers won't know it.
- **Backpack, A 3:** it means "my stuff", not "view and play".
- **Shelf, D 3:** "shelf" is a common interface word (ChromeOS calls its taskbar the Shelf), and a "Pixel Shelf" extension exists.
- **Spritebook, B 2:** 10 letters long. **Spritebook, E 3:** it only says "sprites".
- **Satchel and Haul, S 2:** hard spelling, or a sound that is easy to confuse (Haul sounds like "hall").

## 3. SMILE & SCRATCH (Alexandra Watkins)

**Source:**

- Watkins's book *Hello, My Name Is Awesome* (2nd ed., 2019) uses two checklists ([publisher](https://bkconnection.com/books/title/Hello-My-Name-Is-Awesome)).
- **SMILE** lists five strengths: Suggestive, Meaningful, Imagery, Legs, Emotional.
- **SCRATCH** lists seven deal breakers: Spelling-challenged, Copycat, Restrictive, Annoying, Tame, Curse of knowledge, Hard to pronounce.
- Her own definitions are in her [guest post on the Verisign blog](https://blog.verisign.com/domain-names/how-strong-is-your-startup-name/) (2018).

## 5. Trademark-style knockout screen (not legal advice)

**What I could check:** I could not query USPTO, EUIPO, WIPO or TMview directly; their search pages need JavaScript or blocked access. These results come from web search, indexed Justia mark pages, app stores and product sites. EU and international coverage is effectively zero.

**"PixelLab" itself:**

- I found no registration for "PixelLab". The nearest is PIXLAB: one filing cancelled ([75693872](https://trademarks.justia.com/756/93/pixlab-75693872.html)), and one live with an owner I didn't confirm ([90663167](https://trademarks.justia.com/906/63/pixlab-90663167.html)).
- PixelLab can still have unregistered (common-law) rights in its name.
- Unrelated apps already use the name: "PixelLab - Text on pictures" on Android ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/pixellab--text-on-pictures/about)) and "PixelLab: Drip & Text on Photo" on iOS ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1658958601)).

**The brand-owner risk from the drafts still applies:**

- Apple 4.1(c): "You cannot use another developer's icon, brand, or product name in your app's icon or name, without approval from the developer." 5.2.1 also bans "copycat" names ([Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), checked 2026-10-20).
- Google Play: "Don't imply that your app is related to or authorized by someone that it isn't" ([Play impersonation policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888374)).
- A plain web app avoids store review, but not trademark law.
- None of the 16 words fixes this. The second word only decides how *distinct* the app looks. The prefix needs PixelLab's OK for any store release.

## 7. Extendability

The test: does the name still fit if the app adds downloads, folder sync, sharing, or more than viewing?

- **Fits growth well:** Trove, Backpack (carry it anywhere, sync), Binder (folders, sync), Satchel, Haul, Stash, Shelf, Folio (Folio and Shelf fit sharing especially well), Cabinet.
- **Weaker:** Museum (public, display-only), Keepsake (sentimental), Spritebook (sprites only, not tilesets or sounds), Curio, Navigator.
- **Restrictive:** Reel and Zoetrope (animation only).
- This matches the trade-off Keller et al. describe: the more a name describes, the harder it is to stretch later.

**Stress tests** (base weights):

## Recommendation

**#1: PixelLab Trove.**

- Short name: **Trove**.
- Subtitle: "an unofficial gallery for your PixelLab art".
- Give it its own icon (for example, a small open treasure box, not PixelLab's logo).
- Footer: "Not made by, affiliated with, or endorsed by PixelLab."

| | Name | Why | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **PixelLab Trove** | The only clear winner. Suggestive "treasured collection", clearly not official, 5 letters, stretches to any feature, no gallery-app conflict. | Many non-native speakers don't know "trove", so the subtitle must say "gallery". Trove (the gamigo MMO) is known to game developers. |
| 2 | **PixelLab Backpack** | Easiest to say worldwide, stretches furthest (downloads, sync), clearly fan-made, a strong icon. | The Backpack crypto wallet and exchange is a big same-store neighbor, and the word says "storage" more than "gallery". |

**Same for all three:** ask PixelLab for written OK before any App Store or Play release (Apple 4.1(c)). The fallback form "Trove for PixelLab" keeps the identity if they say no.
